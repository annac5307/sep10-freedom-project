# Entry 2

### Summary 
Through the Google search "Fiction writing apps", the technologies I found were software such as <a href="https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview">Scrivener</a>, and <a href="https://www.novelist.app/">Novelist</a>. These apps offer various features, including ones that allow the user to move around plot points and that let you make books up to no limit. I also discovered written sources like <a href= "https://j-nelson.net/2017/04/computer-programming-writing-fiction-iterative-processes/">Computer programming & writing fiction: Iterative processes | Jim Nelson</a>, which provided a thoughtful perspective on the overlaps in the process of coding and in writing. 

I later revised my Google search to "Fiction writing technology" to obtain more diverse results. One of the displayed websites was <a href="https://kadavy.net/distraction-free-writing-devices/">Kadavy</a>, which listed many different hardware. The first object listed was the **typewriter** which has both mechanical and electrical types. Both types aid in minimizing distractions, although the <a href="https://i5.walmartimages.com/seo/Nakajima-USA-WPT-160-Electronic-Portable-Typewriter-with-Display-White_7d5abae2-0ca2-491b-a060-c643ed00b7e4.3130eb924392fc9b2a9ac53da1ebd7db.jpeg">electrical typewriter</a> in particular requires less finger strength, and uses an electrical motor to power the typing stroke, so it needs to be plugged into an outlet with the correct voltage. Another hardware discussed was the <a href="https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/71IVE7DcX-L.jpg">AlphaSmart</a>, a portable word processor built solely for raw text entry. Any text written in the AlphaSmart can be transferred to a computer or tablet using a USB cable, and it requires just three AA batteries at a time. 

### Organization
Categories other than software and hardware that I considered were difficulty level and price. However, I realized that sorting by difficulty wasn't a good idea since most of the technology I found had similar features, meaning that how challenging they were to use is too subjective. This led to the price being the final organization for my research because it's objective.

### Engineering Design Process
I'm currently in the second stage of the Engineering Design Process (research the problem). Previously in the first stage (define the problem), I identified the issue that people often consider technology to be a threat to writers instead of a tool.

I researched this problem in stage two through discovering various software and hardware that expanded on the potential causes of the problem. After my searches, it's clear to me that different types of technology have their respective purposes in the field of fiction writing. 

The software is typically more helpful in aspects like:
 * Formatting
 * Organizing plot points
 * Editing text
 
Whereas the hardware mainly helps eliminate distraction through its more limited functions and better portability. 

This research has shown me that the issue defined in the first stage could've been caused by the large amount of creative freedom present in the very nature of fiction writing. To elaborate, fiction writing software typically only assists to the extent of structuring while the rest is up to the writer themselves. 

Hardware is even _more_ hands-off (as ironic as it may sound) in the sense that they're designed primarily to just let the writer write drafts in peace. As a result, people could view the carefree essence of fiction writing as a reason why AI can easily infiltrate the field. 

#### Next Steps 
In the third stage of the Engineering Design Process (brainstorm possible solutions), I plan to use my knowledge from my research and from prior experiences to conceive ways that could possibly lessen the wedge between fiction writers and technology by emphasizing what authors have that bots lack.

#### Finding suitable organization styles
As discussed earlier, I ultimately decided to sort the software and hardware I'd found by price due to its objectivity. Although I had to disregard the idea of organizing by difficulty level in the process, because I didn't know how I could determine that quality when many features of the technology were shared. For example, <a href="https://www.novelist.app/">Novelist</a> and <a href="https://www.atticus.io/">Atticus</a> both have the option of using custom templates and pre-made templates, and while <a href="https://www.atticus.io/">Atticus</a> undeniably possesses more features, it could be argued that less features leads to less confusion for writers. This means that any judgment on the difficulty levels of the technology would've come down to personal biases rather than on a general consensus.

This experience with finding an organization style taught me the importance of considering the nature of your topic. Creative writing, including fiction writing, is largely **subjective**. Consequently, the technology that aids in such topics will also have a lot of open room for varying perspectives, so it'd be more accurate to sort the technology in a way which wouldn't contradict with their content.
